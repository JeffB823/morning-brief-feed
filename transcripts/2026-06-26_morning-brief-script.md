HOST: The practical consequence is that Columbus is now in the harder phase of the surveillance debate. It is no longer only about whether cameras help solve crimes. It is about governance. How do you write a defensible policy, verify who can search what, and prove that restrictions are real when the underlying vendor architecture still leaves blind spots. That affects not just privacy advocates and immigrant communities, but also city leaders who do not want a public-safety tool to become a political liability or a compliance scandal.
