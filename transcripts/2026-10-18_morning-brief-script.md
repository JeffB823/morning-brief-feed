CHAPTER: National News
HOST: Across the country Saturday, the No Kings network held events aimed at getting people to vote early in the midterm elections. The Associated Press reports that the network planned hundreds of events and framed them as nonpartisan, even though they grew out of opposition to President Trump. The immediate shift is from gathering people in public to trying to turn that energy into ballots.
COHOST: Does a crowd at a rally tell us who will actually vote?
HOST: Not reliably. A rally can recruit volunteers and remind people about voting, but a turnout claim needs election data, not a head count. Rules and deadlines also differ by state. The relevant test is whether the campaign can reach eligible voters who would otherwise stay home, not whether a photograph looks impressive.
HOST: On the same Saturday, President Trump rallied Republican candidates in Highland Heights, Kentucky. AP reports he was there to bolster turnout, including for House candidate Ed Gallrein. His appearance and the No Kings events are separate efforts, but both demonstrate the premium campaigns place on mobilization before Election Day, November third.
COHOST: So an early vote is not automatically an extra vote for a party. It might simply be a vote that would have arrived later. The distinction matters when campaigns point to early numbers as proof of a final result. A surge in one place also cannot tell us how another county will behave, or how many election-day voters will show up there.
HOST: Correct. Early returns can indicate participation, but they do not establish the outcome. Election officials still have to process ballots, and a campaign's supporters may vote on different schedules. Watch actual certified results and county-level participation rather than reading a national winner from one day's event.

CHAPTER: Home Lending
HOST: First Horizon is changing its mortgage balance sheet: the bank disclosed Thursday that it agreed in early October to sell about four and a half billion dollars of lower-yielding consumer mortgages. It recorded a pretax loss associated with the pending sale. This is a portfolio decision affecting existing loans, not a new rule for a borrower applying today.
COHOST: Why would a bank accept a loss on loans that are still paying?
HOST: The bank says the lower-yielding mortgages have limited value to its current strategy. Selling at a discount recognizes a loss now but can free balance-sheet capacity for other uses. That future earnings benefit is the bank's expectation, not a guarantee. The decision depends on the sale settling and on what the bank does with the proceeds.
COHOST: Does a sale change the borrower's interest rate or mean the loan is in trouble?
HOST: The announcement alone establishes neither. A loan sale is different from a delinquency finding, and borrowers should look for official notices about any change in who handles payments. The useful lender question is whether holding a low-rate asset still makes economic sense given the institution's funding and alternative uses for capital.
HOST: First Horizon said on its earnings call it expects settlement early in the fourth quarter. Watch for confirmation of completion and the bank's subsequent results. The original disclosure was October fifteenth; it is an explicitly dated update, not a new sale announced this morning.
CHAPTER: AI in Banking
HOST: On October fifteenth, FIS said its anti-money-laundering investigations agent became commercially available to U.S. banks, and that it is being deployed at design partner Amalgamated Bank. That changes our tracking from a pilot-oriented vendor announcement to commercial availability and a named deployment. The evidence here is the vendor's announcement, not an independent outcome study.
COHOST: What job is the agent supposed to do, and what should we compare it with?
HOST: It assembles information for financial-crime investigations, including initial alert review, deeper cases and suspicious activity report narratives. FIS says Amalgamated analyzed its current workflows against the agent's expected abilities. The projected reductions are more than ninety percent for initial review, approximately ninety-eight percent for deeper investigation, and more than eighty percent for narrative preparation.
COHOST: Those are striking figures, but are they measured results from completed investigations?
HOST: No. They are projections based on an analysis of current work and expected abilities. The announcement does not establish realized time savings, detection quality, or missed suspicious activity in production. Those distinctions matter because making an investigator faster is useful only if the investigation remains sound.
HOST: FIS says every output is a recommendation and an analyst must confirm a case closure. For a large bank, the immediate decision is whether to evaluate the product against its own alert workload and evidence requirements. The use-case-specific test is whether the narrative accurately traces each assertion back to the source record; a polished summary that omits a material transaction can waste the very time it appears to save.
COHOST: A sensible comparison would sample cases before and after introduction, checking both review time and whether investigators reach supported decisions. Until that evidence is available, the announced projections are a hypothesis about operational capacity, not a staffing plan.
CHAPTER: Frontier AI

COHOST: Does unchanged pricing mean the workflow is unchanged too?
HOST: No. The price and call format are separate from output quality. The company's change log establishes the version change, but it does not by itself prove that a particular bank's document extraction or coding task has improved. Save a representative test set and compare results from the earlier and newer versions before relying on a change in behavior.
HOST: Search access is another capability change: an application can ask the service for current information rather than relying only on what the model already knows. DeepSeek describes its own search quality in its documentation; those are provider claims, not an independent test of every query. Search results can be incomplete or misleading, and the date and source of a retrieved document remain important.
COHOST: That makes the baseline concrete. Compare the same questions, source choices and citations before and after the update, and inspect the cases where an answer changes. A fluent answer alone does not tell an analyst whether the underlying source is current.
HOST: The earliest plausible bank use is a controlled evaluation with approved data; external search and model access would need the institution's relevant data and vendor approval before sensitive work. The update's practical significance is version management, not proof that a benchmark result transfers to a live process.
