Home Lending

Host: In home lending, today's operational change is ARM counseling, not another generic rate recap. MBA says mortgage applications fell 1.5 percent for the week ending September 18, while the average conforming 30-year fixed rate in its survey reached 7.12 percent.

Co-host: Which loan workflow is affected?

Host: Purchase and refinance files where the fixed-rate payment no longer fits. MBA says the ARM share rose to 9.8 percent of applications because 5/1 ARM rates were more than one percentage point below fixed rates. That changes how loan officers explain payment risk, qualification and lock strategy.

Co-host: A lower ARM payment can help a borrower qualify, but what has to be handled carefully?

Host: The borrower needs a clear comparison: initial payment, adjustment date, caps, index, margin, worst-case reset path and whether the household could still afford the loan if rates are higher when the fixed period ends. The operational workflow is suitability, disclosure, secondary pricing and follow-up, not just quoting a cheaper monthly number.

Co-host: And refinance demand tells the same story from the other side?

Host: Yes. MBA says refinance applications fell 3 percent for the week and hit their slowest pace since February 2025. That means lenders cannot rely on refi volume to fill capacity. The useful consequence is pipeline triage: purchase files with payment stress get reviewed first, and ARM education has to happen before a borrower mistakes a temporary payment advantage for permanent affordability.

Co-host: What would change the takeaway?

Host: If long yields ease and fixed rates come down, the ARM share could cool quickly. If fixed quotes stay above 7 percent, lenders should expect more borrowers to ask for ARM options, builder buydowns and seller credits in the same conversation.
