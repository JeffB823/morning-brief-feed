AI in Banking

That places the issue in lane one, consumer servicing and contact-center-adjacent account experience, with a security and liability boundary.

Co-host: What evidence rank does that deserve, and what was measured?

Host: This is reputable reporting on bank terms and industry practice, not a regulator finding, independent test, or disclosed production metric. No source in this report provides a comparable rate of agent errors, unauthorized transactions, fraud losses, successful reversals, customer complaints, or human-review workload. So the evidence supports a narrow statement about responsibility language, not a claim that banks have solved agent authentication or that customer agents are broadly safe.

Host: It changes the customer-permission design question: can the bank distinguish a person’s instruction from an automated tool’s instruction, state the consequences clearly, and handle a disputed action fairly? The limitation is sharp: shifting contractual responsibility does not reveal whether the authorization signal was meaningful or whether a vulnerable customer understood the delegation.

Co-host: The useful takeaway for customers is modest: do not assume an assistant’s convenience changes who must verify an instruction.
