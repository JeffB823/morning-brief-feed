Home Lending

Host: In home lending, the operational change is Desktop Underwriter behavior this weekend. Fannie Mae's DU version 12.1 September update applies to loan casefiles submitted or resubmitted after the evening of September 25.

Co-host: Which file does that change first?

Host: Any conventional file where DU findings drive underwriting conditions, fraud review, collateral review or income validation. The update changes the Potential Red Flag section, adds occupancy and subject-property messages, and uses Collateral Underwriter information for new eligibility messages.

Co-host: Give me the practical underwriter version.

Host: If an appraisal is tied to an appraiser on Fannie Mae's No Longer Accepted list, DU can issue an Ineligible recommendation and require a new appraisal. If the property condition is C6, DU also issues Ineligible. And if CU flags heightened overvaluation risk, the lender gets a message to review whether value is supported.

Host: Income validation also changes. For fixed base income, DU moves from requiring 12 months of current employment to at least three months of current employment plus 12 months of total employment history. Variable base income still needs 12 months of current employment.

Co-host: That sounds like eligibility, integrations and training all at once.

Host: It is. Fannie also says subject rental income on a one-unit principal residence or second home will now make a casefile Ineligible, except for accessory dwelling unit income on a one-unit principal residence. Integrations parsing DU messages may need updates because the impact memo lists new, modified and retired messages.

Co-host: So the borrower may hear only that a condition changed, but the lender has to know whether DU changed the file, the data field, the appraisal path or the income validation clock.

Host: Correct. The watch is Monday pipeline cleanup: how many resubmitted files throw new red flags, ineligible findings or integration defects after the weekend release.

Co-host: The risk is highest for teams that treat automated underwriting like a static black box. When the findings report changes, processors, underwriters, appraisers and technology vendors all need the same map of what the message means.
