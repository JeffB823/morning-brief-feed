Axios Columbus reported the tentative average residential increase is roughly 9 percent this cycle, a slower rise than the last run-up but still a material one for a county where prices have kept climbing even with more inventory on the market.

COHOST: And this is where a housing story stops being a chart and turns into a kitchen-table event.

So homeowners are getting one message from the resale market and another from the county at the same time: there is a little more breathing room in listing activity, but not enough to produce real affordability relief.

A higher value notice does not automatically mean your tax bill rises by the same percentage because levy structure and district-wide changes matter. For local governments and schools, it also raises the political sensitivity around levy fatigue and tax confusion even when the mechanics are more complicated than a straight one-to-one increase.

COHOST: So the immediate move for homeowners is not outrage first. It is read the notice, compare it against nearby sale comps, and understand whether the number looks defendable before the review window gets crowded.

HOST: On AI and technology, the latest useful model story is not a single giant launch. It is a cleaner split in how companies should think about model routing. Google said on June 3 that Gemma 4 12B is a unified, encoder-free multimodal model designed to bring laptop-scale agentic work closer to the edge, including native audio input. Google says it can run in a reduced memory footprint on consumer hardware, which is the important operational point.

COHOST: Because the real question is not whether a model sounds impressive. It is whether a real team can deploy it without sending every document, recording, or customer interaction into a frontier cloud workflow by default.

HOST: Exactly. For mortgage, banking, legal, and compliance-heavy teams, that matters. A local or near-local multimodal model changes the approval conversation for tasks like document triage, call summarization, lightweight research, and internal workflow support. You still need access controls, auditability, and human review. But the privacy, latency, and cost tradeoff starts to look more practical when more work can stay closer to the device or the enterprise boundary.

HOST: At the premium end, Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 is still the stronger signal for heavier reasoning and longer agentic work. Anthropic says the new version improves capability while fast mode runs two and a half times faster and costs one-third as much as prior versions in that mode. For engineering and operations teams, that matters because it cuts into two of the biggest objections to using advanced models in production: waiting too long and paying too much for longer tasks.

COHOST: And OpenAI's latest move was almost the opposite kind of signal. Less a huge new model reveal, more a cleanup of the menu and memory behavior around the product.

HOST: Right. OpenAI's release notes updated June 4 say memory in ChatGPT is being refreshed to stay more up to date for U.S. Plus and Pro users, and the May 28 notes say GPT-4.5 will be retired from ChatGPT on June 27, 2026 while OpenAI o3 will be retired from ChatGPT on August 26, 2026. Those are ChatGPT-side changes, not API retirements, but the practical signal is clear. Vendors are still simplifying front-end model choices even while capability keeps improving underneath.

HOST: Put together, the more informed enterprise takeaway is this. The market is separating into three lanes. Smaller multimodal models for privacy-sensitive or cost-sensitive work. Frontier reasoning models for high-value, longer-horizon tasks. And product simplification on top, because too many overlapping model choices create governance drag faster than they create value. The counter-signal is that local models still give up some quality and frontier models still bring concentration and oversight risk. But the next watch item is whether more companies write explicit routing rules this month instead of letting employees improvise model choice tool by tool.
